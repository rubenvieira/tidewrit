# Pouring and dripping

Pour and drip add new sand through the same deposit pass as water (`water.md`); they and bucket molds add sand to the bed. Each deposit adds a Gaussian mound whose volume is exactly `sand · π · radius² / 4`, so the pour rate is a volume per second (1e-5 m³/s at the default size, scaling with size²), independent of frame rate. The GPU test checks the poured volume to 1%.

**Pour** lands a continuous stream along the timestamped pointer path. Each segment uses the analytic line average of the Gaussian, retaining its volume while spreading it over the distance actually traveled. A fast drag therefore leaves an unbroken line even when only one deposit fits the frame budget. Equal-velocity coalesced events merge; bends, changes of speed, and stationary intervals retain their measured residence time. A still pointer grows a cone that avalanches at the angle of repose, and slower moving pointers naturally lay thicker lines. The visible stream is light grains spawned by the particle shader up to 8 cm above each emitter, falling under gravity with drag. They carry only about 1 mg-equivalent each (well under 1% of the poured volume), so the visual never competes with the deposit for mass. Emitters (at most 8, after symmetry) and a per-inactive-grain spawn chance are passed in a `pour` array in the simulation parameters. The chance is sized for about 40 grains per substep per emitter.

**Drip** releases slurry blobs at 7 per second, each 2.5e-7 m³ at the default size (scaling with size³), scattered by up to a third of a blob radius. Drips carry moisture just past saturation (1.05). Soaked slurry was tried first: it is the least cohesive state, so each drip flattened the top before it could set and the result was a flat-topped block. Just past saturation, a drip slumps slightly and its film drains within steps, leaving damp sand that stands vertical faces, so drips stack into lumpy towers. The GPU test checks that ten wet drips stand at least 2 mm taller than the same sand dropped dry.

A heightfield at 1.56 mm cells cannot hold overhangs or delicate spires; drip castles read as stacked lumpy mounds.

Pointer release preserves unsubmitted sand, so a drip tapped between animation frames still lands once and a short pour adds its actual held duration. Release timestamps share the animation clock and only the interval after the last submission is added. Released deposits capture their size and symmetry, stop visual emitters immediately, and wait for enough room to submit their complete symmetry group. Drip sound is triggered on submission, never merely on release. Canceling a pointer discards its unsubmitted releases; clearing the tool discards all pending deposits and sounds.

The release queue retains at most 64 source deposits, dropping the oldest on overflow to favor recent input. Held pointers rotate through limited frame budgets and retain at most 100 ms of continuous pour or two due drips. This bounds recovery after a stall or a frame filled by water drops, without accumulating a later sand burst.

Continuous paths additionally retain at most 64 segments per held pointer. Trimming stale work clips both its time and geometry; it never deposits the discarded interval at the newest position. Sweeps keep the existing 48-deposit frame limit and use the existing deposit pass. Their Gaussian integral uses complementary-error-function tails for numerical stability, with the same support cutoff and point-source limit as stationary pouring. Untimed programmatic moves preserve endpoint behavior; browser input supplies normalized timestamps.
