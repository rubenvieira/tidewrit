# Digging and bucket molds

**Bed depth.** The bed is 8 cm deep, up from 3.2 cm, so holes can reach the water table. The camera eye is defined as a fixed height (0.488 m) above the bed surface, so the depth change did not move the view. Everything else (initial heights, wave reset, water reset, lighting, grains) was already relative to `SAND.depth`.

**Digging conserves sand exactly.** `excavate` removes a bowl (1 − r²/R²)² under each dig, limited by the sand actually present (never closer than 2 mm to the floor), and sums each dig's removal in fixed-point atomics. `spoil` then spreads exactly that total over a truncated Gaussian heap (cut at 3σ). Its normalization is the discrete sum of those same cell weights, so where a scoop bottoms out never matters and the GPU test conserves to 2e-6 of the bed. The scoop rate is a volume per second (3e-5 m³/s at the default size, ∝ size²), and the bowl's peak depth comes from its π·R²/3 volume. Wall slumping into the hole is ordinary transport, so holding still digs a crater whose walls keep caving in, and dragging digs a trench.

Moving scoops integrate that quartic bowl analytically along each timestamped path segment, and the spoil uses the matching swept Gaussian on the far bank. Long gestures retain continuous trench walls without spending extra operations on spatial subdivision: even eightfold symmetry stays within the existing eight-scoop frame cap and the same two passes. Equal-velocity samples merge; bends and changes of speed remain separate so geometry and residence time survive coalesced input. Each held pointer keeps only the newest 100 ms and at most 64 segments. Trimming work also trims its path, preventing a delayed scoop burst after a stall.

Spoil normalization runs on the GPU during the existing excavation pass, over the combined scoop and spoil bounds. Each 8×8 workgroup reduces removed volume and quantized heap weights before publishing its totals, avoiding global atomic contention per cell. The spoil pass uses that identical quantized weight in both numerator and denominator; precision scales with heap size and requested volume to prevent integer overflow. This preserves conservation at tray edges and eliminates CPU grid scans for long or mirrored sweeps without adding a pass or readback.

**Spoil** lands 3.6 sizes from the scoop: to the right and slightly away from the viewer when still, and on the far bank of a moving trench (to the right of the dig heading). Thrown spoil is loose, not packed, so it carries only enough moisture (0.1) for about twice the dry cohesion; at the subsurface's full dampness it stacked into a needle. While digging, spoil points join the pour emitters, so thrown sand visibly falls onto the heap.

**Damp subsurface.** Sand more than 2 cm below the original surface counts as damp subsurface sand (0.35, blended over 1.5 cm). A deep hole therefore has a sloping dry funnel at the top and near-vertical damp walls below it, like a real beach hole. Finger strokes never reach that depth, so drawing is unaffected.

**Groundwater.** The hydrate pass treats anything below the water table (4 cm under the original surface) as flooded: moisture rises to a standing film proportional to the depth below the table, so pools appear in deep holes and moats. Within a 12 mm capillary fringe above it, sand stays damp. Seen from above, water reflects only about 2% of the sky, so pools read dark like real ones. Their mirror reflection is not occluded by the hole walls, though: a pool the camera can see also sees open sky.

**Molds** raise packed damp sand (moisture 0.55) to the ground level under their center plus their shape, keeping existing sand where it is already higher. A bucket placed beside a tower therefore merges with it rather than doubling their shared wall (additive stacking made exactly such ridges), while a bucket placed on top of a tower stacks, as a real one would. The ground level is read in its own pass before any mold writes. They are the only tools besides pour and drip that add sand. Shapes live in `footprints.ts` as (outline distance, height in mold radii), so the hover outline is exactly the mold: a tapered bucket tower with a crenellated parapet, a walled castle with corner turrets and a keep, a knobbly starfish, a turtle with grooved shell plates, and a scallop. Parts are only as tall as damp cohesion can hold; taller thin turrets slumped into cones.

**Packed damp sand is a weak solid.** Damp sand stands a vertical face up to 3 cm on top of its granular slope, the holding-height cohesion described in `kinetic.md`. An earlier slope multiplier (4×, then 12×) let every wall steeper than its angle flow on the first frames: crenellations melted and turrets slumped into cones within two seconds. Tools still yield it, because bonds under a tool's core are severed, so a finger cuts straight through a castle. The GPU test checks both that crenellations survive and that a stroke cuts the tower below half height. Flooded sand holds nothing, so flooding still slumps it.

Two moisture rules keep sculptures damp until they truly dry. Capillary wicking between neighboring cells needs grain contact, so it fades with their height difference (e-folding over 2 mm): a castle wall touches the dry beach only at its foot and no longer dries through its sides within a second. Moisture also describes a 5 mm surface layer, so sand added on top (spoil, pour, drips before their own water) mixes in by volume; loose sand piled on a damp mold therefore stays loose instead of inheriting the mold's packing. Damp evaporation is 150 s, so castles keep their edges for a few minutes and then sag and crumble as they dry, as real ones do.

Short shovel gestures preserve the elapsed interval through pointer release even when no animation frame occurred while held. Only unsubmitted time is retained, capped at 100 ms; release stops spoil emitters immediately. Finished scoops capture their geometry and symmetry and submit whole groups within the existing per-frame dig limit. Held pointers take turns when their combined symmetry groups exceed that limit.

Released scoops and bucket molds each retain at most 64 pending gestures, favoring recent input on overflow. Molds capture symmetry when placed. Scoop and mold thuds accompany simulation submission, so blocked work stays silent until it lands; tool cancellation clears pending work and sounds.
