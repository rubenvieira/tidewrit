# Water

Moisture is a per-cell field (0 dry, 1 saturated, above 1 a standing film, capped at 1.6) in its own ping-pong buffer pair that follows the sand state index. It is not packed into the state vector because every state lane is already used, and a separate pass keeps the transport kernel within the eight-storage-buffer default limit (transport reads moisture as its eighth binding).

**Landing.** The water tool never makes a contact. Deposits are shared with pour and drip, which add Gaussian sand mounds through the same pass (`pouring.md`). `WaterThrower` turns gestures into drops with landing times (tap: pool plus droplets; drag: trickle every 0.9 size; flick above 0.35 m/s: an elongated splat thrown ahead, with flight time growing with distance). Landed drops are uploaded once per frame (at most 48, after symmetry) and dispatched only over their bounding box.

**Impact conservation.** A splash moves sand through paired transfers on the four axial grid links. The outward pressure is Gaussian, scaled so its discrete divergence approaches the established -(1 - r²)·exp(-r²) pit and rim when the drop is resolved. Overlapping pressure fields are summed before choosing each link's direction. All outgoing links share the sand available above the floor, and physical tray edges allow no outward flow. A depleted bed therefore cannot create a rim without first removing its sand, and a clipped or subcell drop cannot change total bed volume. The final height has no floor clamp that could hide a conservation failure. This remains a local impact approximation, not fluid erosion or a full water momentum solver.

Impact batches add one conditional prepass bounded only by the impact endpoints and their Gaussian supports, with a one-cell halo for receivers beyond that support. A simultaneous long swept pour expands the deposit pass without expanding impact work. The prepass borrows the ordinary solver's axial flux lanes; the deposit pass gathers only when its own cell and the contributing neighbor lie inside the impact bounds, and the next granular step overwrites the scratch. Pure pour and moisture-only batches retain their single pass, with no new buffer or readback. Each drop is rejected outside its support plus that halo before Gaussian evaluation, and the CPU prepares its scalar coefficients without scanning grid cells. GPU regressions cover empty and thin beds, tiny drops, all tray sides and a corner, overlapping fields, stale scratch, unchanged plain sand deposits, and a resolved pit profile within 6% of its original depth scale. An instrumented GPU regression also verifies that a small splash keeps the same impact dispatch when combined with a long pour, and produces the same bed as the isolated operations even with stale scratch across the rest of the deposit region.

**Transport.** After each integrate, incoming sand mixes by volume into a local 5 mm surface reference, matching pour, spoil, and packing. Pushing dry sand over a wet surface therefore dilutes its moisture just as depositing that same volume does, regardless of the total bed depth. The hydrate pass then adds symmetric capillary diffusion (weakened by height difference between neighbors) and film runoff down the water-surface gradient (bed height plus 0.5 mm per film unit). Damp moisture decays with a 150 s time constant, while film soaks in with a 4 s one. Below the water table, groundwater keeps holes flooded (`earthworks.md`).

Moisture is a surface-composition proxy, not a conserved water inventory for the full sand column. Outgoing sand carries the source's current composition; removing it alone leaves that surface composition unchanged, approximating similar material immediately underneath. Burial, exposure, saturation capping, drying, and groundwater do not track a separate subsurface water store. The existing capillary and runoff exchanges remain symmetric per link.

**Behavior.** Damp sand gains cohesion: it stands vertical faces up to 3 cm, so damp cuts hold vertical walls and molded and drip castles stand (see `kinetic.md` and `earthworks.md`), holds steeper walls and suppresses grain spray. Past saturation the threshold drops below dry, but runoff drains peaks within a few steps, so a soaked mound turns damp and holds. Slumping only occurs where water actually pools (flooded groove walls). This is deliberate and matches real sand. Dry sand absorbs arriving water up to saturation before any film forms, so puddles collect in grooves only once the surroundings are wet.

**Rendering.** The surface shader samples moisture bilinearly. Damp darkens albedo and reduces glints. Film flattens grain relief, drops roughness to a mirror, and uses a water Fresnel term.

The wave reset dries the rows it rebuilds, and a full reset clears moisture.

**Input lifecycle.** Each airborne source drop belongs to its pointer and captures size and symmetry when emitted. Canceling that pointer removes its unsubmitted tap, trickle, and thrown drops while preserving other pointers and sounds whose deposits already submitted. A landing waits until its whole symmetry group fits the frame budget. Tap and trickle sounds accompany their central deposit; a thrown sheet sounds with its middle body deposit. Deferred or canceled drops therefore cannot produce a premature splash. Clearing all water input also clears undrained sounds.

**Input bounds.** The pending queue retains the most recent 256 source drops and the sound queue retains 64 submitted splashes. Velocity history keeps at most 64 samples within the existing 80 ms window. Captured positions are bounded to the bed, and one move emits at most 64 trickle samples, retaining the newest portion and carrying only the spacing remainder. Long jumps and stalled rendering cannot leave an unbounded sampling loop or a growing backlog; ordinary tap, trickle, and flick patterns keep their existing spacing and flight delays.
