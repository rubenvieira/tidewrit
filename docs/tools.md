# Sand tools

Every tool except water is a contact shape inside the existing swept-contact pipeline, not a separate solver (water is in `water.md`). Each contact writes its kind and an angle into the spare `end.zw` lanes of its uniform slot, and `sampleTool` evaluates the matching footprint, which returns a normalized distance (it drives the disturbed shell) and a coverage (it scales penetration). Transport, ejection and settling stay unchanged, so all tools conserve mass and keep the same contact-field cost: one footprint evaluation per contact per cell, after a reach-based reject.

One size slider drives every tool. `TOOL_SHAPE` maps it to each contact's outer reach (written as the contact radius, so grain ejection sampling covers the whole tool), to the narrowest feature along the stroke (it bounds distance resampling), and to depth: the rake scales pointer pressure, while the trowel and shell use a fixed depth because the tool itself sets it.

The tool is latched per pointer at pointer down, so switching tools mid-gesture never changes a live stroke.

Footprints live in `simulation/footprints.ts` and are compiled into both the solver and the surface shader. The hover outline is the footprint's distance-1 contour, drawn at every symmetry image, so the preview is exactly the contact the bed will receive (including the next shell's pre-rolled orientation and the rake's hover heading).

- **Rake**: seven capsule tines that share one swept segment. Each cell resolves to its nearest tine across the stroke direction, so curves stay continuous and the rake costs one contact rather than seven, which keeps the eight-contact batch available for multi-touch.
- **Smooth (trowel)**: a wide blade whose depth ramps from zero at the leading edge to full at the trailing edge. Neighbor transport moves sand at most about one cell per substep (≈ 0.19 m/s at 512² and 120 Hz). A flat blade only diffused ridges sideways; the ramp makes every covered cell shed forward, so excess rides ahead as a bow wave and drops into grooves. Sweeps faster than the transport speed leave streaks; that is accepted rather than adding an advection pass.
- **Shell**: a stationary scallop imprint at a random orientation. Since a stationary press only evacuates through the footprint gradient, the shape is held for `SHELL_PRESS_STEPS` substeps (0.4 s) so the imprint reaches full depth. Presses live outside the pointer tracks, contribute one contact per batch, and are capped at half the contact batch so they can never starve concurrent strokes. Rib depth is kept under the repose slope so the ribs survive settling.
- **Broom**: thirteen fine bristles on the rake's tined footprint, shallow, with a fixed per-bristle stiffness so a sweep leaves uneven streaks. Bristles are narrower than the repose slope can hold, so streaks soften into a brushed texture rather than staying as grooves.
- **Wind**: a soft round brush whose depth pattern is a ripple train: gentle upwind (stoss) slopes and steep lee faces, with depth in the troughs so transport heaps the crests. Phase is locked to world position along the gesture's wind heading, latched from its first segment, so overlapping passes reinforce the same crests instead of scrambling them. Low-frequency meander bends the crests along their length. The wavelength is 1.1 size, about eight cells at the default size; much shorter ripples would fall below what the grid resolves.
- **Feet**: stationary presses like the shell (30 substeps each), placed by the queue every 8 sizes of travelled distance, alternating sides 1.9 size from the path. The first step lands after a quarter stride, once the heading is known, and a tap leaves a single print facing up the screen. The footprint is eight weighted elliptical pads (heel, ball, outer edge, five toes) with the inner arch not touching. Handedness travels in the contact's `motion.w` lane and flips under mirror symmetry. Narrower gaits overlap neighbouring prints into unreadable chains, which is why the stride and offset are as wide as they are.

All contact tools share one parameter layout: `end.z` kind, `end.w` orientation (shell, foot, wind heading, all with heading (-sin a, cos a)), `motion.w` variant. The footprint function also receives the absolute cell position, for world-locked patterns.
